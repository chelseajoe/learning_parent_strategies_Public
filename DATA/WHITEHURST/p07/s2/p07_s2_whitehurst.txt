Explanation:
The parent engages well with the child, frequently prompting them to participate in reading. However, the types of prompts used could be more varied to better align with the CROWD acronym. Evaluations are performed but are not always clear or reinforcing, expansions happen but lack consistency and depth, and repetition of prompts after expansions is virtually absent.

How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
Ask the child more open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
Example: "Why do you think the townspeople don't have food stores?"

Incorporate Recall Prompts:
Help the child understand the story plot and describe sequences of events.
Example: "Can you tell me what happened when the pancake flew through the air?"

Utilize Distancing Prompts:
Ask the child to relate the story to their own experiences.
Example: "Have you ever had a day where it rained a lot? What did it feel like?"

Provide Clear Evaluations:
Acknowledge and reinforce the child's correct responses.
Example: "Yes, that's right. It is a pancake! Good job."

Expand More Frequently and In-Depth:
Rephrase or add more detailed information to the child's responses to enrich vocabulary and understanding.
Example: "Yes, it's a fog. A fog is like a cloud that's close to the ground and makes it hard to see."

Repeat Prompts After Expansions:
Ensure the child has learned new vocabulary or information by repeating the prompts.
Example: "Can you say 'fog' again for me? Remember, it's like a cloud that's close to the ground."

By incorporating these suggestions, the parent can better align with PEER dialogic reading standards and further the child’s language development and comprehension skills.
