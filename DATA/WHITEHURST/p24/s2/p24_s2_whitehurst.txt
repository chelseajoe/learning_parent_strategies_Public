Explanation:
The parent can significantly improve in their engagement with the child during the reading session. While there are occasional prompts and expansions, they are not consistently used to enhance the child’s learning experience. The evaluation of responses and repetition of prompts are notably scarce and would benefit from more deliberate usage.

How the Parent Can Improve:

- Use More Engaging Prompts:
  Incorporate more varied and engaging prompts beyond simple yes/no questions or prompts for action.
  Example: "Why do you think Gloria makes the children laugh?"

- Evaluate Responses Clearly:
  Make sure to evaluate the child's responses by acknowledging when they are correct and providing positive reinforcement.
  Example: 
  Child: "That's a dog!"
  Parent: "Yes, that's right! It's a police dog named Gloria. Good job!"

- Expand on Responses Meaningfully:
  Add more depth to the child's responses by providing additional information or rephrasing what the child has said.
  Example: 
  Parent: "That's correct, it's a dog. Gloria is a police dog who helps Officer Buckle. Can you say police dog?"

- Repeat Prompts to Reinforce Learning:
  Revisit prompts to reinforce learning and ensure the child has understood the new information.
  Example: 
  Parent: "Remember how Gloria obeys Officer Buckle? What was one of the safety tips they taught?"

- Incorporate CROWD Prompts:
  Use a variety of CROWD prompts to engage the child further in the story:
  - Completion Prompt: “Officer Buckle’s safety tip number seventy-seven is never stand on a swivel ___.”
  - Recall Prompt: “Do you remember what happened at the school when Gloria visited?”
  - Open-ended Prompt: “What do you think Gloria is doing in this picture?”
  - Wh- Prompt: “Why do you think the children started listening to Officer Buckle’s tips?”
  - Distancing Prompt: “Can you think of a time when you had to remember to be safe like Gloria and Officer Buckle?”
