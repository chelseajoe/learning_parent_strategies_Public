    Explanation:
    The parent needs significant improvement in all areas of the PEER sequence. Prompting is not varied and robust; evaluations are mostly acknowledgments rather than substantive assessments or encouragements. Expansions are rare and not comprehensive, and the parent doesn't repeat prompts to reinforce learning.

    How the Parent Can Improve:

    Use More Engaging Prompts:
    - Ask the child more open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
    - Example: "Why do you think the Lorax was taken away?"

    Incorporate Recall Prompts:
    - Ask the child to remember and recount parts of the story.
    - Example: "Can you tell me what happened to the Bar-ba-loots?"

    Utilize Wh-Prompts:
    - Use more 'wh-' questions to understand the child's perspective and encourage detailed responses.
    - Example: "What do you think will happen next?"

    Practice Expansion:
    - Rephrase the child's responses and add additional information.
    - Example: Child says, "Lorax." Parent responds, "Yes, that's the Lorax. The Lorax speaks for the trees because they have no tongues."

    Repeat the Prompt:
    - After expansion, repeat the initial question to ensure understanding.
    - Example: "Can you say Lorax again for me?"

    Engage with Distancing Prompts:
    - Connect the story to the child's real-life experiences.
    - Example: "Have you ever seen a tree that you thought needed protection?"

    Encourage Participation:
    - Foster more active participation from the child throughout the reading session.
    - Example: "Can you help me read the next page?"

By incorporating these techniques, the parent will be better aligned with dialogic reading practices and help improve the child’s linguistic and cognitive skills through more effective interactions.
