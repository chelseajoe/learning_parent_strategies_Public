Explanation:
The parent does well in prompting the child and frequently evaluating responses. However, they lack depth in expansions and hardly ever repeat prompts post-expansion to reinforce learning.

How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
- Ask the child more open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
- Example: "Why do you think Harold decided to use a crayon for his walk?"

Evaluate More Specifically:
- Instead of just saying “Good”, provide specific feedback that ties back to the story or introduces new concepts.
- Example: “Yes, a boat! Boats help people stay afloat in the water.”

Expand More Deeply:
- Add layers of information or ask further questions to build on the child’s responses.
- Example: After the child says "Wave," the parent could say, “Yes, waves in the ocean. Waves can be big and small. Have you ever seen waves at the beach?”

Repeat Prompts to Reinforce Learning:
- Revisit prompts after expansions to ensure the child has grasped the concepts.
- Example: “You mentioned Harold used a crayon to draw the tree. Can you remember why he used the crayon?”

By incorporating more of these dialogic reading strategies, the parent can enhance the child’s engagement and comprehension, fostering a more enriching reading experience.
