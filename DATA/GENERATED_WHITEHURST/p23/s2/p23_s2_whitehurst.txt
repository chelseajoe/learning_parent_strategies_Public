The parent mostly provides simple prompts and evaluations and does some expansion but rarely repeats the prompts. There's a significant opportunity to improve in all four PEER areas by using more engaging, varied, and consistent dialogic reading practices.

How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
- Ask the child open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
- Example: "Why do you think the baby bird wants to find his mother so badly?"

Incorporate Recall Prompts:
- Engage the child by asking about what happened earlier in the story to reinforce comprehension.
- Example: "Do you remember what the baby bird said when he saw the kitten?"

Use Completion Prompts:
- Encourage the child to complete the sentences to improve their grasp of language structure.
- Example: "The egg jumped, it jumped, and it..."

Increase Evaluations:
- More frequent and detailed evaluations will help the child know when they are correct and provide encouragement.
- Example: "That’s right! The baby bird could not fly, but he could walk. Great job!"

Expand More Frequently and Thoroughly:
- After the child responds, add more information to their responses to deepen understanding.
- Example: "He didn't see his mother because he went by her. Maybe he didn't know what she looked like and was too focused on finding her quickly."

Repeat the Prompt:
- After expanding on the child’s response, revisit the original prompt to reinforce learning.
- Example: "That’s right. Can you say it again? 'He could walk.'"
