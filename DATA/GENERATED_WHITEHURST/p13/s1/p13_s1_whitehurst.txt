Explanation:
The parent can improve significantly in all areas of PEER. While they do engage in reading with the child, their adherence to good dialogic reading practices is limited. They need to use more dynamic and varied questioning techniques and must create more opportunities for the child to actively engage in the storytelling process.

How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
- Ask the child more open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
- Example: "Why do you think Harry wanted to run away when he heard the bath water?"

Incorporate CROWD prompts to enhance engagement and deeper understanding:
  - Completion prompts: "Harry was a white dog with black ___ (spots)."
  - Recall prompts: "Can you tell me what happened when Harry got dirty?"
  - Open-ended prompts: "What do you think is going on in this picture?"
  - Wh- prompts: "Why do you think Harry's family didn't recognize him?"
  - Distancing prompts: "Have you ever felt like Harry? When was a time you didn't want to do something like take a bath?"

Evaluate More Consistently:
- Provide clear evaluations to the child’s responses to validate or correct and explain further.
- Example: "Yes, that’s right, Harry didn't want to take a bath because he didn’t like it. Baths can sometimes feel like a lot of work, can’t they?"

Expand Responses:
- Take the child's simple answers and expand on them by adding information or rephrasing.
- Example: Child: "That's a spoon." Parent: "Yes, that's right! It's a big shiny spoon. We use spoons to eat many things, like soup and cereals."

Repeat Prompts for Reinforcement:
- After correcting or expanding on a response, repeat the prompt to reinforce learning.
- Example: Parent: "Right, he didn't want a bath because he wanted to keep playing and getting dirty. Can you say why Harry didn't want a bath?"
