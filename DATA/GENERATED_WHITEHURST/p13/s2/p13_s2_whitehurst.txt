    Explanation:
    The parent primarily focuses on correcting the child's pronunciation and does not sufficiently use dialogic reading strategies to prompt, evaluate, expand, or repeat effectively. This interaction could be greatly enhanced by engaging more with the content and helping the child make connections between the story and their own experiences. 

    How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
    - Ask the child more open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
    - Example: "Why do you think the Grinch wants to stop Christmas?" (Open-ended prompt)

Incorporate Recall Prompts:
    - Regularly ask questions that help the child recall what has happened in the story so far.
    - Example: "Do you remember what the Grinch did first to stop Christmas?" (Recall prompt)

Ask WH-questions:
    - Use WH-questions to encourage the child to describe and explain elements of the story.
    - Example: "What do you think is the Grinch's plan now?" (Wh- prompt)

Incorporate Distancing Prompts:
    - Encourage the child to relate the story to their own life experiences.
    - Example: "Have you ever felt like you didn’t want to do something everyone else was excited about?" (Distancing prompt)

Evaluate More Effectively:
    - Acknowledge the child’s responses with specific praise and feedback to build confidence.
    - Example: "That's right! You remembered it perfectly. Good job!"

Expand More on Responses:
    - Add more information to the child’s responses to deepen their understanding.
    - Example: "Yes, it’s a reindeer! They have big antlers, which help them find food in the snow."

Repeating the Prompt:
    - After expanding on the child's response, repeat the prompt to reinforce learning.
    - Example: "Can you tell me why the Grinch's heart grew three sizes?"
