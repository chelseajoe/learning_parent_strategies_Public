Explanation:
The parent generally engages the child and encourages interaction, however, they can improve in several areas to make the reading session more beneficial. The parent could incorporate more varied prompts and be more deliberate in evaluating, expanding, and repeating information.

How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
- Ask the child more open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
- Example: "Why do you think Camilla turned into a rainbow?"

Evaluate the Child’s Responses:
- Give more affirmative evaluations to the child's responses to validate their understanding.
- Example: “That’s correct, those green things are bacteria tails. Great observation!”

Expand the Child’s Responses:
- Expand on the child’s answers to add more depth and context.
- Example: “Yes, that is mashed potatoes. It looks like they are having a mashed potato storm – what else can you spot in the mashed potatoes?”

Repeat the Prompt:
- Revisit the prompt after expanding to reinforce learning.
- Example: “Remember earlier when we talked about contagious? Can you tell me what it means again?”

Incorporate the CROWD Prompts:

Completion prompts:
- Incorporate fill-in-the-blank questions to help with language structure.
- Example: “Camilla wanted to fit in, so she decided to...”

Recall prompts:
- Ask questions about the story plot and sequences of events.
- Example: “What did Camilla look like after she ate the beans?”

Open-ended prompts:
- Use prompts that focus on the pictures to engage the child's imagination.
- Example: “Tell me what’s happening in this picture where Camilla is a rainbow?”

Wh- prompts:
- Use questions starting with what, where, when, why, and how to introduce new vocabulary.
- Example: “Why do you think the kids laughed at Camilla?”

Distancing prompts:
- Relate the story events to real-life experiences.
- Example: “Have you ever had to take medicine that tasted bad like Camilla?”
