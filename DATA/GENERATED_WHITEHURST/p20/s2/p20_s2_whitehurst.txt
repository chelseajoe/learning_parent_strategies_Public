Explanation:
The parent incorporates some elements of the PEER method, particularly Prompting and Evaluating. However, they lack depth in Expanding the child's responses and do not consistently Repeat prompts to reinforce learning.

How the Parent Can Improve:

Use More Engaging Prompts:
- Ask open-ended questions that require critical thinking and imagination.
- Example: "Why do you think Gloria the dog is important for safety tips?"

Incorporate Completion Prompts:
- Ask the child to fill in the blanks.
- Example: "Officer Buckle gave safety tip number one: Keep your shoelaces ___. How should they be?"

Incorporate Recall Prompts:
- Encourage the child to remember and explain past events in the story.
- Example: "Do you remember what happened when Officer Buckle first brought Gloria to the school?"

Ask More Wh- Prompts:
- Use who, what, where, when, why, and how questions to build vocabulary.
- Example: "What do you think will happen next?"

Incorporate Distancing Prompts:
- Help the child connect the story to their own experiences.
- Example: "Have you ever seen a dog like Gloria? What was it doing?"

Expand Responses More Deeply:
- Add descriptive details and information to expand the child's responses.
- Example: If the child says, "He fell down," expand with, "Yes, he fell because his shoelace was untied, and that’s why Officer Buckle had the rule about keeping shoelaces tied."

Repeat the Prompts:
- After expanding their response, repeat the initial question to ensure understanding.
- Example: "So, what should you always do with your shoelaces?"
