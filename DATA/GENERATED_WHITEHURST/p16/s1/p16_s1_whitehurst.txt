How the Parent Can Improve:

1. Use More Engaging Prompts:
   - Ask more open-ended questions to stimulate imagination and critical thinking.
   - Example: "Why do you think the boy comes back to the tree?" (Open-ended prompt)

2. Evaluate Responses Clearly:
   - Offer more explicit evaluations to affirm the child's answers.
   - Example: "That's right, it's an apple tree because it has apples on it." (Evaluation)

3. Expand on Child's Responses:
   - Build on what the child says by adding more information or rephrasing.
   - Example: After the child identifies an apple tree, continue with, "Yes, and those apples look ripe and delicious. Sometimes, people pick apples and make them into pies. Have you ever eaten an apple pie?" (Expansion and Distancing)

4. Repeat Prompts to Reinforce Learning:
   - Reiterate prompts and information to ensure retention.
   - Example: After expanding, ask, "Can you say apple tree again? Remember, trees with apples are called apple trees." (Repetition)
